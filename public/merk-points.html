Merk Points
Shop local · Earn a merk

A community concept

Shop local. Earn a stake in Shetland's shared life.

Merk Points is a simple idea: earn a merk for every pound spent with a local business, and redeem them for the things that make island life richer — a swim, a film, a museum visit, a bus fare. Funded not by shops, but by the community bodies whose very purpose is to help folk take part.

Spend local — and Shetland gives back.

A concept note · OneShetland · Darren Fullerton Consultancy Ltd

An island-wide loyalty scheme where shopping local earns you a share of Shetland's shared life — and no business ever pays for another's reward.

Ordinary loyalty keeps you coming back to one shop. Merk Points does something bigger: it rewards spending local anywhere in the isles, and pays that reward out through the pools, museums, cinemas and buses the community already runs.

The name: a merk is a Shetland unit of worth with deep Norse roots. Under the udal land law the isles kept from their centuries under Norway — until Shetland passed to the Scottish Crown in 1469 — land across Shetland was valued and rented in merks, and a Scots silver merk later circulated as a coin. It's a fitting name for a modern way to reward keeping value in Shetland — though a merk here is a reward, never cash.

It's a concept — but it runs on loyalty rails OneShetland has already built and tested: a member card, a loyalty engine, and Gift-Aided community donations. What's needed to make it real isn't new technology; it's one or two willing reward partners.

The currency

Meet the merk

One merk for every pound spent local.

Merks aren't cash and can't be withdrawn — they're redeemed for island experiences, so their worth stays in Shetland.

How it works

Three simple moves

1

Earn

Shoppers earn one merk for every £1 spent with a participating local business. Issuing a merk costs the shop nothing — so every firm, however small or remote, can join for free.

2

Fund

Community partners — the council and Shetland's trusts — back the rewards. Not as charity, but because getting folk swimming, into the museum, on the bus is exactly what they exist to do.

3

Redeem

Points unlock real island experiences at those partners' venues. Loyalty stops being money off at a till and becomes a stake in island life.

Why it's fair

Nobody loses

✕The trap we avoid

In a shop-only points scheme, whoever honours a point loses margin for a reward its rivals handed out. The popular shops end up subsidising everyone else — and quietly pull out. That's why those schemes collapse.

✓Why this is different

Here the reward is honoured by a community partner whose mission is participation — a swim, a film, a visit. Honouring a point is the very thing they exist to do. Businesses only ever issue points, for free. No business subsidises another.

The practical side

Built to be safe and simple

Points, not cash

Points can't be withdrawn or swapped for money — so it's a loyalty scheme, not stored value, and stays clear of the e-money rules a cash wallet would raise.

No runaway cost

Points can expire and be capped, and the funding partner sets a budgeted pot and a simple rate — so the cost is always known, never open-ended.

The rails already exist

OneShetland already runs a loyalty engine, a member card and Gift-Aided donations. This is an extension we can pilot quickly — not a build from scratch.

The ask

How we'd start — small and measured

Merk Points doesn't need every partner at once. It needs one willing reward partner and a focused pilot. If it works, it grows.

  • A short conversationHalf an hour to walk through the idea and see where our aims meet.
  • Pick one flagship rewardA single, recognisable prize — say a swim, or museum entry — to launch with.
  • Agree a simple rateHow many merks unlock it — for example, 250 merks for a swim.
  • Budget a capped potA small, measurable amount to fund the trial, with no open-ended liability.
  • Run a pilotOne facility or one town, over a season, measured together.
  • Grow with partnersAdd trusts and rewards once the first pilot has proven itself.
